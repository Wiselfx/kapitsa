KFlash batch budget: batched decode's verify rows chosen every cycle from the members' calibrated drafts and the measured cost (M4 Pro, 3 at once: 35B +16-20%, 27B +15-23%; 2 and 4 at once -1 to +4%; a request alone unchanged)

With 2-4 requests decoding at once on a DFlash2 drafter, Engine::batch_rows gave every member a fixed row count (2 at
once 4 each, 3 or 4 at once 2 each, the 27B's 3 taking turns). Now KfBatch (src/model/kflash_budget.h) decides each
cycle, after the members' drafting passes, how many rows the batched verify checks in all (members .. 8) and which member
gets them:
- a draft's worth is its calibrated survival: the product over its chain of sigmoid(alpha_d + beta_d logit(c_j)), c_j
  the largest of the 16 candidates' probabilities under the walk's scores. For a greedy member that is its chain node's
  q, the tree's own quantity: KfBudget's calibration, fed the batched chains' records too (KfBudget::records). A sampled
  member (exact speculative sampling) is another event: KfBatch's own calibration. c_j does not move with the draw at j;
- every member verifies its anchor, then drafts go in decreasing survival over the batch while each pays
  (survival >= rate x the cost of one more row), so each member's rows are a prefix of its chain and a draft's place
  depends only on the drafts ranked before it: the sampled members' speculative sampling stays exact;
- cost: a batched cycle's GPU time by members and rows, measured and cached like KfBudget's (KfCosts, extracted from
  KfBudget unchanged), read as a robust line in rows, every 64th cycle re-measuring the stalest count (a table measured
  while the GPU was busy once locked the rule into plain rows).
The decision runs on the host: the batched verify's per-member dispatches (GDN, attention partials, captures, the
sampler) take their rows and row offsets when encoded, so GPU-written counts would mean changing ~10 kernels. The verify
is encoded once the drafting passes are done and its first layer is committed at once (encode_verify_multi's
split_after): the GPU waits 0.18-0.31 ms per batched cycle for the host. NI_BATCH_KF=0 keeps the table;
NI_BATCH_KF_FLOOR=1 gives every member a draft (measured once: -2.5 to -3.9% at 4 at once on the 35B; off).
Bolt's LiLiCorr keeps its table.
