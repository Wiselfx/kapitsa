Flash-Next Q2_0 streamed on a 48 GB M4 Pro: the automatic expert store kept within 40% of the RAM (15-24 GB stores made every command buffer 0.5-3.5 s slower; at 12 GB decode runs 16-18 tok/s), each request's store reads in the log, sparse-table rows --stream

What changed:
- src/model/qwen4_exp.cpp: the automatic store size (NI_EXPERT_STORE unset) keeps the process's GPU
  memory within 40% of the RAM (and at least three layers' experts), with a log line when it cuts the
  budget's size. On this Mac the budget left a 29.9 GB store; the cap gives 12.0 GB. NI_EXPERT_STORE=<GB>
  still sets any size.
- src/engine/engine.cpp: with streamed experts, each request logs its prompt's and its decode's store work
  (experts looked up, in memory, read ahead, read at the turn, GB read, MB a token).
- src/model/expert_store.cpp: the load line names the largest buffer size. src/engine/doctor.cpp: the
  streamed line names the cap.
- tools/qwen4_exp_q20.py: `rows --stream` / `convert --stream` cut the sparse table's rows from whole
  16 MB pieces of its tensors instead of three ~0.5 s CDN reads a row (test: the same file either way).
- docs/usage.md: the cap, the log, and the numbers below (it said the 48 GB speed was not measured).

Why the cap: a store short of all the experts waits on the CPU at every layer. ni_run, MTP depth 2, the
harness's decode prompt, 16 tokens, GPU ms per cycle by store size: 4 GB 247, 6 GB 226, 8 GB 210,
10 GB 197, 12 GB 187; 15 GB 743, 18 GB 2968 (two 9 GB buffers 1762), 24 GB (two 12 GB) 3696; plain
decode at 18 GB 3004-5025 ms a step against 102-118 at 6-10 GB. The turn thread's own waits stayed at
~1 ms a layer; a `sample` of the slow runs shows Metal's submission thread blocked in
IOGPUCommandQueueSubmitCommandBuffers in 361 of 366 samples. The server's GPU memory of 18.9 GB (39% of
48 GB) and 20.4 GB (4096-row chunks) ran at full speed; ni_run with a 15 GB store (~21-22 GB) did not.
Not an interleaved A/B: the effect is 4-16x, measured in two sessions on one Mac; where the threshold
lies on other Macs or under other memory loads is not known.

Runs: MacBook Pro Mac16,7, M4 Pro (20-core GPU), 48 GB, macOS 27.0.1 (26A434), AC; kapitsa serve,
32K context, 1 slot, --dflash none, the harness's protocol (bench/harness/bench.py, seeded nonces and
"seed": 1234 so the prompts repeat), a 12 GB store, NI_MEM_BUDGET 23. The pack: tools/qwen4_exp_q20.py
from nace-ai/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-MLX-4bit @ 5d423f9f and ISTA-DASLab/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-GSQ-RCO-GGUF @
ed59f920 (ranged reads; `check`: every layer's expert bytes equal the GGUF's), with a sparse n-gram table

Decode found 88-92% of its experts in memory and read 105-200 MB a token; the least free memory in any
timed run was 45% (21.6 GB). The decode answers matched their table rows for their first 70-158 tokens
(76-95% of the positions had every row); rows the table lacks read as zero, so the tokens after the
first such position are not the model's own, and the full table would add its rows' reads. An earlier pass with less of the
answers' rows wrote 20.1 / 18.9 tok/s (MTP greedy / T = 0.6) and 18.8 / 16.7 plain, on more repetitive
text that reused experts.

Prefill profile (ni_run, NI_PROFILE=1, 12 GB store, the 4K prompt): 11.7 s of isolated GPU time, of it
dense 4-bit GEMMs (DeltaNet, shared expert) 34%, Q2_0 expert GEMMs 28%, 6-bit GEMMs (attention,
hyper-connections) 15%, attention 4%, DeltaNet scan 3%, hyper-connection mixes 4%; the n-gram gather
35-39 ms a 2048-row chunk on the CPU. A chunk reads nearly every expert (38-41 GB), so 2048-row chunks
wait on the SSD (20.0 s, 77 GB read) and 4096-row chunks are about GPU-bound (12.1 s, 41 GB). The
decode test's 85-token prompts read every expert too (24-31 GB, 5.9-7.7 s to the first token): not
changed here.
